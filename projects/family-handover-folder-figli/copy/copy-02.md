My dad answered in two seconds flat. That's what scared me.

I'd asked him, kind of as a joke, where his passport was. "Top drawer, left side, under the Christmas cards." Instant. No hesitation.

So the next week, when he was at his cardiologist appointment, I went looking for it myself. Just to see.

It wasn't there.

It was in a folder, in a different drawer, under a stack of old insurance paperwork from three moves ago. He'd just... remembered it wrong, or it had moved, or both. I don't know. What I know is I stood there at 11pm with a flashlight on my phone, pulling open drawer after drawer in my parents' den, feeling my chest get tight for no reason I could name yet.

I called my sister. She said the usual: "Dad's sharp as a tack, he knows exactly where everything is." And he does. That's the part nobody tells you.

It's not that he doesn't know. It's that what he knows only exists in his head, in that specific moment, in that specific mood. Nobody else can open that drawer when he's not the one standing in front of it.

That's when it actually clicked for me, at almost midnight, sitting on his office floor: this was never a memory problem. It was a location problem. The information was real, it was accurate, it just lived in exactly one place that only works if he's awake, calm, and standing right there to retrieve it.

Try it yourself this week. Ask whoever's "got it handled" in your family to tell you, right now, where one specific thing actually is. Time it. Don't go looking — just listen to how confident the answer sounds, and then go check.

I'm not writing this to scare anyone. I just wish someone had told me sooner that being organized in your head isn't the same as being findable by someone else. That one distinction changed what I did next.
