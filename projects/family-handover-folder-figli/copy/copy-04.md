It was 11:40pm when I opened a spreadsheet I hadn't touched in eight months.

My mom had fallen getting out of the shower. Nothing broken, thank god. But the hospital kept asking questions I couldn't answer — her insurance group number, who her doctor was, whether she had a living will on file anywhere.

I remembered I'd made a file for exactly this. "Mom's Stuff," sitting in my Google Drive since March.

I opened it with shaking hands, half relieved.

It was useless.

Half the fields were blank. The bank listed wasn't even the one she used anymore — she'd switched accounts sometime over the summer and never told me, because why would she, it wasn't a big deal to her.

I sat on the edge of the hospital chair and just stared at it. I remember thinking, I'm not lazy. I built this thing. I tried.

So why did it feel like it had rotted while I wasn't looking.

That's the part that kept me up after we got home that night. It wasn't that I forgot to update it. It's that nothing ever told me when to. Her life kept quietly changing — a new bank, a new doctor, a password reset — and the spreadsheet just sat there, looking finished, while the truth moved on without it.

The information wasn't hidden from me. My mom wasn't keeping secrets. It was just scattered — a little in her head, a little in a drawer, a little in an account I didn't know existed yet. A document I build once can't chase that. It needs somewhere designed to catch the changes, not just record a snapshot from eight months ago.

I'm not writing this to scare anyone. I just wish someone had told me the spreadsheet wasn't the problem — the fact that nothing was keeping it alive was.

If you've got one sitting in a drive somewhere too, go check it tonight. Not tomorrow.
