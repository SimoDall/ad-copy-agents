## Note di tracciabilità (italiano)

- **Desiderio di Massa:** "Voglio lasciare tutto in un unico posto, pronto da trovare."
- **Livello di Consapevolezza:** Problem Aware.
- **Angolo usato:** ANGOLO 7 (L4) — "Averlo scritto da qualche parte non significa che qualcuno lo troverà." Credenza attuale ("ho già scritto delle cose, sono avanti") → Nuova informazione (scritto ≠ trovabile, scoperte per caso) → Riformulazione (illusione di sicurezza, più rischiosa del non aver scritto nulla) → Conclusione naturale (serve UN unico punto noto in anticipo a tutta la famiglia).
- **Meccanismo usato:** UMP/UMS #3 — "Il Bersaglio Mobile" (The Moving Target), Livello 3: l'organizzazione non è un'azione singola ma un'abitudine; un elenco di informazioni è come una previsione del tempo, esatta il giorno in cui la scrivi e silenziosamente sbagliata col tempo. La soluzione (aggiornamenti gratuiti a vita) la mantiene vera nel tempo invece di farla invecchiare come una fotografia.
- La copy intreccia i due meccanismi: la protagonista scopre prima che "scritto" non vuol dire "trovabile" (Angolo 7), poi — scavando più a fondo nella propria scoperta — realizza che anche se qualcuno trovasse le sue note, buona parte sarebbe già superata (Meccanismo 3). La soluzione risolve entrambi: un unico punto conosciuto in anticipo, mantenuto aggiornato nel tempo.

**Headline:** I Had It All Written Down Somewhere. That Was Never the Problem.

**Testo primario (Facebook Ads):**

My neighbor Carol got a letter last spring addressed to her husband. He'd been gone eleven months.

It was from an insurance company he'd apparently forgotten to mention. Nobody went looking for it. It just showed up, in an envelope with his name on it, like he was still there to open it.

I couldn't stop thinking about that letter.

Because I'd already done "the responsible thing." A note on my phone with our account numbers. A half-finished email draft to my daughter titled "important stuff, just in case." A password jotted in the back of my address book.

I felt ahead of the game, honestly. Most people I know haven't even started.

Then one night, well past 2am, I couldn't sleep after Carol told me about that letter. I sat at the kitchen table and tried something simple. I asked myself: if something happened to me tomorrow, would my husband even know these notes existed?

He wouldn't. Not because he isn't paying attention. Because I never told him there was anything to look for.

That's when it hit me — "written down" and "findable" are two completely different things. I'd solved the first one and never noticed I'd skipped the second.

Then came the part that really unsettled me. I pulled up that phone note to check it. Half of it was already wrong. One account I'd closed two years ago. A password I'd changed last spring and never updated anywhere. I hadn't organized anything once and been done — I'd just taken a photograph of a moment that had already moved on without me.

What I needed wasn't more notes in more places. It was one place, known in advance, and kept honest as life kept changing.

So I built one properly — already divided into sections, so I wasn't starting from a blank page again, with free updates for life so it never quietly goes stale the way my old notes did.

I still think about Carol's letter. Eleven months, and a stranger's envelope told her what she needed to know, not a conversation, not a note.

I just wanted to share what finally made sense to me — in case it helps someone else stop feeling "ahead" and start actually being found.
