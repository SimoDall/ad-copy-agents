# Copy 04

## Headline
Your Will Doesn't Tell Anyone Where Anything Is. I Found That Out At 2 AM.

## Primary Text

My mother's will was perfect. Her lawyer called it "textbook."

It still took us three weeks to find out which bank even held her savings.

I remember sitting on her kitchen floor at 11 PM, surrounded by folders. A folder for the house. A folder for the policies. A folder marked "bank stuff." Every single one labeled in her neat handwriting.

I kept thinking: she was so organized. Why can't we find anything?

We called her lawyer again. He read us the will a second time. Who gets the house. Who gets the savings. Who gets what.

Nobody could tell us where the savings account actually was.

That's when it hit me, somewhere around 2 AM, half-asleep on her couch with a flashlight and a shoebox of old statements: the will had done exactly what it was built to do. It just was never built to do the thing we actually needed.

A will answers one question — who inherits. It was never written to answer the question we were really asking, at midnight, exhausted: where is it, right now, today?

Her folders weren't the problem either. They were tidy. What was missing was the one page that said "folder A connects to folder B, here's the safe, here's the lawyer's number." Without that page, four tidy folders are just four separate islands. Only she knew the bridges between them.

I started my own folder that week. Not another will. Not another binder with blank pages staring back at me.

One single page. Everything that matters, and exactly where to find it — written down so my own kids never spend a night on someone's kitchen floor wondering why "organized" didn't mean "findable."

If you've already got a will and figure that covers it, it doesn't. It was never meant to.
