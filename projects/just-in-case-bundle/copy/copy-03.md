My sister asked me one question. I didn't have an answer. Neither did anyone else.

It was a Tuesday. I was at urgent care with a kidney infection, half-listening to a nurse, when my phone buzzed. My sister, watching my kids for two hours: "Does she take her inhaler before or after dinner?"

I didn't know she needed to know that. I thought I'd covered it — I'd texted her the allergies, the school pickup time, the pediatrician's number. The important stuff.

Lying on that exam table, I started mentally listing everyone who calls me first. The school. The pediatrician. My mother-in-law when she's unsure about nap time. My husband, from the grocery store, texting "wait, which snack is the one that's okay?"

Every single one of them. Me.

I'd always called that being organized. That night I realized it's not a compliment — it's a single point of failure. If I'm out of reach for a day, the whole house doesn't know what to do. Not because no one cares. Because no one else holds the file.

Here's what actually clicked, at 2am, scrolling my own Notes app looking for "the list" I swore I'd made: my notes aren't information. They're reminders. "Inhaler — after" means something to me because I already have the whole scene in my head. To my sister, standing in my kitchen, it's a word with no story attached.

I hadn't under-written anything. I'd written for someone who already knew everything I know.

So I rebuilt it differently — not by topic, the way I'd think about it, but by the questions someone would actually ask walking in cold: what do you need in the first two hours, what do you need tonight, what can wait till this week.

That's the whole shift. Not more effort from me. A document written for the person who wasn't there.

The Just-In-Case Bundle is that document, already built — $34, fillable on your phone in one sitting, handed to whoever steps in next.

I'm not sharing this because something went wrong. I'm sharing it because I got lucky it was only a kidney infection, and a question about an inhaler. Next time I might not be the one who gets to answer.
